The lowest-friction conversion path

Turns untrackable SWS footfall into a CRM contact and a first online order
Step 1
Customer in a SWS store

57% of touchpoints cannot fulfil online. These shoppers are anonymous today, with no CPF and no CRM contact.

Step 2
Scans the QR code

The highest-satisfaction touchpoint in the whole network at +98.5 NPS. Already a digital habit.

Step 3
Registers a CPF

The scan page becomes the registration gateway. This is the step that makes the customer addressable at all.

Step 4
Receives a price-led offer

Pricing is the number one physical complaint at 24.9%, so a discount offer hits their actual pain point.

Step 5
Click & Collect at a nearby LOJAS

79% of SWS sit within 5 km of an O2O store, median 1.8 km. Highest online NPS at +57.3, and it bypasses delivery entirely.

Each step draws on a separate finding: QR satisfaction, the CPF identification gap, pricing complaints, store proximity and the NPS ranking by fulfilment mode. Together, these findings support the proposed journey.

Updated seasonal finding. In the refreshed 25-month series, online share peaks in June and July and reaches its low from November through January. December 2024 records the series minimum at 3.82%. The pattern was checked independently within each year.

December has the highest absolute sales but the lowest online share. High total demand therefore does not translate into stronger online adoption. The conversion campaign should follow channel share rather than the overall revenue peak.

Physical peaks at the month boundary, with day 1 and day 30 both about 11% above average. Online peaks about a week later, on days 6 and 7, at 18% to 20% above average. Both channels reach their low on days 24 and 25. Day 31 is low only because fewer months include it.

Barbecue is a weekend-gathering pattern: Saturday is roughly 3× Monday, with Friday the clear lead-in. Everyday demand also peaks Saturday but far more gently, about 30% above its flattest day. The shape holds across every WHO band, so it is universal rather than band-specific.

Average daily revenue on holidays versus non-holidays. Online falls about 32 percent on holidays while physical rises about 24 percent.

Online revenue is lower on holidays for every day of the week without exception. Physical rises, though that uplift is likely concentrated in a few major holidays such as Christmas and Carnaval rather than spread evenly.

Campaign effect

Campaigns lift baskets, not channel mix

Inside campaign windows both channels lift almost identically: physical basket +35% (R$33.84 → R$45.57), online +37% (R$42.04 → R$57.70).

ZVEC share still falls from 4.75% to 4.42% because physical records about 27 transactions for every online transaction during campaign windows. Campaign timing increases basket value but does not shift the channel mix. An online-specific mechanic is needed to do that.

The pipeline

How 150M rows become the numbers on these pages
  1. Read selectively. Queries run in DuckDB directly against partitioned Parquet files using glob patterns. Only the columns a question needs are read; the full dataset is never loaded into memory.
  2. Clean. Internal bonus entries (BNFC), null document rows, and business-to-business document types (ZVFS, ZVAM, ZZPJ) are excluded from consumer analysis. SALES_BY_DOCUMENT cleans from 150,861,282 to 150,413,704 rows.
  3. Respect the snapshot grain. CUSTOMER_BEHAVIOR is a rolling 12-month monthly snapshot, so monthly files are never concatenated. A single month is used for point-in-time KPIs, paired months for transition analysis, the full panel only for trends.
  4. Score and validate. The priority score is computed on one month, then validated out-of-time by observing who actually converted the following month.
  5. Aggregate and publish. Only the resulting aggregates are embedded in this page. No customer-level records are shipped to the browser.

Glossary

Portuguese terms and document codes used throughout
Físico
Physical-only customer: buys in store, never online, in the last 12 months. 90.7% of the active base.
Omnichannel
Buys in both a physical store and Swift-online within a rolling 12 months.
O2O
Online-to-offline. A store is O2O-enabled if it can fulfil an online order.
LOJAS
Company-owned Swift stores. All are O2O-enabled.
SWS
Store-within-store, inside partner supermarkets such as Carrefour and GPA. None are O2O-enabled, and their customers cannot be individually tracked.
CPF
Brazilian individual tax ID. Required to link an in-store purchase to a known customer record.
ZPDV
Document code for physical point-of-sale consumer transactions, about 94% of receipts.
ZVEC
Document code for online consumer orders. "ZVEC share" means online share of revenue.
LF / LO
Survey channel codes for the physical store and online journeys.
RFV
Recência, Frequência, Valor. The loyalty scoring system behind the customer tiers.
Tiers
Bronze → Prata (silver) → Ouro (gold) → Gran Reserva → Black, lowest to highest.
Label_Cesta
Basket persona label, the segmentation behind the six personas on the What page.
Churrasco
Brazilian barbecue. A weekend-centred social occasion, and a distinct purchasing pattern.
Dia-a-dia
Everyday staples, as opposed to occasion buying.
Click & Collect
Order online, collect in store. "Retire na Loja".
Lift
Conversion rate of a group divided by the base rate (0.16%/month). A 6.4× lift means 6.4 times more likely to convert.
NPS
Promoter% minus detractor% on a 0 to 10 scale, with a possible range from −100 to +100. It is not an average score.
Out-of-time validation
Score one month, then check who actually converted the next month.
