The core finding

Conversion is the bottleneck, not retention

Only 1.33% of físico-only customers converted over 24 months, while 41% went inactive: a customer is roughly 31× more likely to churn than to convert. But once converted, 97.5% stay omnichannel the following month.

So the budget belongs at the top of the funnel. Retention already works; activation is what fails.

A five-tier ladder built from rules you can read

Every físico customer is scored on five independent axes, then sorted into bands. No machine learning: each input is a named column, and every band is validated out-of-time against who actually converted the following month.

The lever

Click & Collect: +57.3 NPS, lowest detractor rate

"Retire na Loja" is online's best-liked mode and it is exactly the O2O behaviour we want to grow. It sidesteps the delivery complaints that dominate online detractors, and it brings customers into a store where Swift controls the experience.

When to activate

Timing constraints

Payday windows. Activation concentrates on payday and early-month windows using the CALENDAR business-day flag.

3 to 4 day lead time. Campaigns land ahead of the push to respect Swift's stated preparation window.

December ceiling. Seasonal peak is R$89.6M versus R$58.5M in the June trough, so high-effort Priority 1 activation is scheduled pre-December.

Where to deploy

The handoff

One reusable artifact

Every view resolves to the same output, refreshable each month on a new snapshot: customer key, band, PriorityScore, and top drivers. That is what Swift's CRM team needs to run the campaign.
