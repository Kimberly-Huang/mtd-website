Why omnichannel matters

Value gap, stable across the panel

Omnichannel customers spend R$100.78 monthly versus R$29.21 for físico-only, visit 6 times versus 2, and average R$264 per receipt versus R$126.

R$ and visits per month·omni vs físico medians·25-month panel·Source: CUSTOMER_BEHAVIOR · SALES_BY_DOCUMENT

The core finding

Conversion is the bottleneck, not retention

Only 1.33% of físico-only customers converted over 24 months, while 41% went inactive: a customer is roughly 31× more likely to churn than to convert. But once converted, 97.5% stay omnichannel the following month.

So the budget belongs at the top of the funnel. Retention already works; activation is what fails.

57% of touchpoints cannot fulfil online. These shoppers are anonymous today, with no CPF and no CRM contact.

Step 2
Scans the QR code

The highest-satisfaction touchpoint in the whole network at +98.5 NPS. Already a digital habit.

Step 3
Registers a CPF

The scan page becomes the registration gateway. This is the step that makes the customer addressable at all.

Step 4
Receives a price-led offer

Pricing is the number one physical complaint at 24.9%, so a discount offer hits their actual pain point.

Step 5
Click & Collect at a nearby LOJAS

79% of SWS sit within 5 km of an O2O store, median 1.8 km. Highest online NPS at +57.3, and it bypasses delivery entirely.

Every step in this chain is evidenced separately: the QR satisfaction score, the CPF identification gap, the pricing complaint share, the store proximity distribution, and the modality NPS ranking. The path is the join of those five findings, not a new assumption.

ZVEC % share of revenue·n = 150.4M transactions·May 2024 – May 2026·Source: SALES_BY_DOCUMENT

Revised finding. The refreshed 25-month series puts the online-share peak in June–July and the trough in November–January (Dec 2024 = 3.82%, the lowest point in the series). This replaces our earlier assumption of an October–January peak, and it was verified independently within each year rather than pooled.

The two are not in conflict, and the distinction is the point: December has the highest absolute sales but the lowest online share. The month that sells the most is the hardest month to move people online, so the conversion campaign should not chase the revenue peak.

Physical peaks at the month boundary (day 1 and day 30, both about +11%). Online peaks about a week later, in the day 6–7 window (+18 to 20%). Both bottom out at day 24–25. Day 31 is low only because fewer months have one.

Barbecue is a weekend-gathering pattern: Saturday is roughly 3× Monday, with Friday the clear lead-in. Everyday demand also peaks Saturday but far more gently, about 30% above its flattest day. The shape holds across every WHO band, so it is universal rather than band-specific.

Online revenue is lower on holidays for every day of the week without exception. Physical rises, though that uplift is likely concentrated in a few major holidays such as Christmas and Carnaval rather than spread evenly.

The counter-intuitive one

Campaigns lift baskets, not channel mix

Inside campaign windows both channels lift almost identically: physical basket +35% (R$33.84 → R$45.57), online +37% (R$42.04 → R$57.70).

Yet ZVEC share falls from 4.75% to 4.42%, because physical runs about 27 transactions for every 1 online in-window. Timing alone moves value, not channel share. Shifting the mix needs an online-specific mechanic paired with the campaign.

Methods, definitions and limits

What the data can and cannot tell us

Everything on this dashboard rests on one proprietary extract and a set of definitions that need to be explicit, because two of them differ from the sponsor's own reporting. This page states the pipeline, the vocabulary, and the boundaries.

Definition reconciliation

Why we report 2.87% and the sponsor reports 10.82%

Both figures are correct under their own definition, and the gap is definitional rather than an error in either analysis.

This dashboard · 2.87%
A customer counts as omnichannel only if they purchased in both a físico store and Swift-online within a rolling 12 months. 80,281 of 2,799,245 active customers.
Sponsor reporting · 10.82%
A broader O2O definition covering roughly 302K of 2.79M customers. It admits channel interactions that our stricter both-channel purchase test excludes.

We use the stricter definition deliberately: the strategy targets customers who must complete an actual online purchase, so the metric has to move only when that happens. The two figures must be aligned with the sponsor before the final deliverable, since the 6% target was set against their definition, not ours.

The pipeline

How 150M rows become the numbers on these pages
  1. Read selectively. Queries run in DuckDB directly against partitioned Parquet files using glob patterns. Only the columns a question needs are read; the full dataset is never loaded into memory.
  2. Clean. Internal bonus entries (BNFC), null document rows, and business-to-business document types (ZVFS, ZVAM, ZZPJ) are excluded from consumer analysis. SALES_BY_DOCUMENT cleans from 150,861,282 to 150,413,704 rows.
  3. Respect the snapshot grain. CUSTOMER_BEHAVIOR is a rolling 12-month monthly snapshot, so monthly files are never concatenated. A single month is used for point-in-time KPIs, paired months for transition analysis, the full panel only for trends.
  4. Score and validate. The priority score is computed on one month, then validated out-of-time by observing who actually converted the following month.
  5. Aggregate and publish. Only the resulting aggregates are embedded in this page. No customer-level records are shipped to the browser.

Limitations we own

What this analysis structurally cannot answer

Customers cannot be linked to stores

CUSTOMER_BEHAVIOR carries no store or geographic identifier. This is why geography is used as a deployment filter for campaign rollout and never as an input to the individual customer score. Any claim of the form "customers near store X behave like Y" is outside what this data supports.

READINESS partly overlaps the outcome

A prior online purchase is close to the very thing that makes someone omnichannel, so P1's edge is partly true by construction. P1 is best read as the strongest actionable signal, not as a pure prediction. The 730-day cutoff on that axis is a judgement call, disclosed rather than tuned.

NPS is not representative of the base

Survey response rate is roughly 2.7% of active customers, and once split by channel the detractor sample thins further. NPS is used as an experience diagnostic to decide what to fix, never as a per-customer targeting signal. We tested it as a churn predictor and it failed.

Geographic and identity coverage is partial

Only 73 of 125 store cities match GEO_INFORMATION, and 528 of 624 touchpoints have usable coordinates. Separately, 29% of physical receipts are anonymous, and each anonymous visit generates a new ID, so those shoppers cannot be profiled or targeted at all.

Missingness in the behavioural table

About 22 of 84 CUSTOMER_BEHAVIOR columns exceed 50% missingness and are excluded. Recencia_Online is populated for only about 5.9% of físico customers, which makes it a powerful but sparse signal. Age is usable for roughly 60% of customers; missing values default to neutral.

Some findings await sponsor confirmation

The treatment of ZVLV (Retail Store Sales, 1.85% of rows but 25% of revenue) is unresolved, as is whether the QR Code channel connects structurally to SWS locations. The holiday uplift on physical is likely concentrated in a few major holidays rather than spread evenly, and warrants a per-holiday split.

Glossary

Portuguese terms and document codes used throughout
Físico
Physical-only customer: buys in store, never online, in the last 12 months. 90.7% of the active base.
Omnichannel
Buys in both a physical store and Swift-online within a rolling 12 months.
O2O
Online-to-offline. A store is O2O-enabled if it can fulfil an online order.
LOJAS
Company-owned Swift stores. All are O2O-enabled.
SWS
Store-within-store, inside partner supermarkets such as Carrefour and GPA. None are O2O-enabled, and their customers cannot be individually tracked.
CPF
Brazilian individual tax ID. Required to link an in-store purchase to a known customer record.
ZPDV
Document code for physical point-of-sale consumer transactions, about 94% of receipts.
ZVEC
Document code for online consumer orders. "ZVEC share" means online share of revenue.
LF / LO
Survey channel codes for the physical store and online journeys.
RFV
Recência, Frequência, Valor. The loyalty scoring system behind the customer tiers.
Tiers
Bronze → Prata (silver) → Ouro (gold) → Gran Reserva → Black, lowest to highest.
Label_Cesta
Basket persona label, the segmentation behind the six personas on the What page.
Churrasco
Brazilian barbecue. A weekend-centred social occasion, and a distinct purchasing pattern.
Dia-a-dia
Everyday staples, as opposed to occasion buying.
Click & Collect
Order online, collect in store. "Retire na Loja".
Lift
Conversion rate of a group divided by the base rate (0.16%/month). A 6.4× lift means 6.4 times more likely to convert.
NPS
Promoter% minus detractor% on a 0–10 scale, ranging −100 to +100. Not an average score.
Out-of-time validation
Score one month, then check who actually converted the next month.
